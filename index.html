Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana: The Complete Parent's Guide to the SSY Calculator

Few financial decisions feel as emotional as saving for a daughter. The Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, usually shortened to SSY, was created by the Government of India under the Beti Bachao Beti Padhao campaign precisely for that feeling. It turns a modest, regular deposit into a protected corpus for a girl's education and future, and it does so with a government-backed interest rate that has consistently sat above most fixed deposits. The LittleNest Sukanya Samriddhi Calculator exists to make that long journey visible. Instead of a single maturity number dropped on you without context, it draws the whole twenty-one years as a gentle timeline, shows your daughter's age at every step, separates the fifteen deposit years from the six growth-only years, and even estimates the education withdrawal you may rely on when she turns eighteen. Planning becomes less about guessing and more about watching a small nest grow, season after season.

Eligibility is deliberately simple. A parent or legal guardian can open exactly one SSY account for a girl child, and the account must be opened before she turns ten. A family may open accounts for two daughters, and in the special case of twin or triplet girls, more accounts are permitted with supporting certificates. The account can be opened at a post office or an authorised bank branch with the child's birth certificate, the guardian's identity and address proof, and a small first deposit. Once opened, the account belongs to the girl, although the guardian operates it until she turns eighteen. This calculator begins from that same rule: the age you enter must be between zero and ten, because those are the only ages at which a real account can begin, and every projection flows from that honest starting point.

The deposit rules reward consistency rather than wealth. You must deposit at least two hundred and fifty rupees in a financial year, and you may deposit at most one lakh fifty thousand rupees, in multiples of fifty rupees, through as many instalments as you like. Many families deposit monthly, treating SSY like a quiet salary-day ritual, while others deposit a lump sum in April to maximise the interest calculation, since SSY interest is computed on the lowest balance between the fifth day and the end of each month. Deposits are required only for the first fifteen years from opening. After that, the account does not close or stop earning; it simply enters a patient phase in which no fresh money goes in, yet interest keeps compounding until the twenty-first year. That structure is exactly what the year-by-year grid on this page displays, with the phase column flipping from deposit years to growth only at year sixteen.

Interest is where SSY quietly shines. The rate is declared by the Ministry of Finance every quarter, alongside other small savings schemes, and it has historically remained among the highest available to ordinary savers. Interest is calculated monthly but credited to the account once a year, and it compounds annually. Over twenty-one years, compounding creates the familiar snowball: in the early years the interest is small, almost disappointing, but by the final growth-only years the annual interest alone can exceed what you once deposited in a full year. LittleNest models this with annual compounding at the rate you choose, applying interest to the opening balance plus that year's deposit. Because the real rate will change many times over two decades, the calculator invites you to test several rates. Try a cautious figure and an optimistic one, and treat the truth as a band between them rather than a single promise.

Maturity arrives twenty-one years after the account opens. If you open the account for a newborn, she will be twenty-one when it matures, perfectly timed for higher studies or a first step into adult life. If you open it when she is eight, maturity comes at twenty-nine, which changes the plan entirely and might suit a wedding, a business seed fund, or postgraduate study abroad. This is why LittleNest displays her age at maturity so prominently: the right deposit amount depends on what life event the money is meant to meet. The account can also be closed at her marriage after she turns eighteen, with the required declarations, and premature closure is allowed in narrow compassionate circumstances, such as the death of a guardian or a life-threatening medical emergency, usually with an interest penalty. Knowing these exits in advance helps families commit calmly rather than anxiously.

The education withdrawal is the feature parents ask about most, and it deserves careful reading. After the girl turns eighteen, or after she passes Class ten, whichever framework the operating office applies with documents, a partial withdrawal is permitted specifically for higher education. The ceiling is fifty percent of the balance standing at the end of the preceding financial year. The withdrawal is not a single event; it can be taken in one instalment or spread across up to five yearly instalments while the course continues, supported by admission proof and a fee structure from the institution. LittleNest estimates this amount from your own projection, using the balance at the point she turns eighteen, so you can see early whether SSY alone might cover a degree or whether a second savings pot should grow beside it. Treat the estimate as a planning compass, not a bank statement, because actual eligibility is confirmed by the post office or bank when documents are presented.

Tax treatment adds one more layer of generosity. SSY is one of the few instruments in India with exempt-exempt-exempt status. Deposits qualify for deduction under Section 80C up to the overall limit of one lakh fifty thousand rupees a year, the interest credited each year is not taxed, and the maturity proceeds are tax-free as well. For a guardian in a higher tax slab, that triple benefit quietly raises the effective return beyond the headline rate. Rules can change with future budgets, and the new tax regime treats deductions differently, so a quick conversation with a tax adviser is worthwhile before committing the maximum amount. Still, even ignoring tax entirely, the combination of sovereign backing, a strong declared rate, and a twenty-one-year compounding runway makes SSY difficult to beat for a goal that is genuinely long term.

How much should you actually deposit? Start from the goal and work backwards. Estimate what a degree might cost when your daughter reaches eighteen, remembering that education inflation in India has often run higher than general inflation. Enter that thinking into the calculator in reverse: try the maximum deposit and note the maturity value, then lower the deposit until the projection feels both useful and sustainable. A plan you can hold for fifteen years beats an ambitious plan abandoned in year four, because a missed year attracts a small default fee and the account must be revived with arrears before it counts again. Many parents align the deposit with a monthly figure they barely notice, which is why this page converts your yearly deposit into a monthly equivalent. Fifty thousand rupees a year sounds large; a little over four thousand a month sounds like a habit, and habits are what fifteen-year plans are built from.

It also helps to place SSY honestly among its companions. A Public Provident Fund account offers a longer flexible tenure and suits retirement-style goals, but it is not dedicated to a daughter and its rate has generally trailed SSY. Equity mutual funds through systematic investment plans can outpace SSY across long horizons, yet they carry market risk and no guaranteed floor, which many parents dislike for a non-negotiable education goal. Fixed deposits are simpler but are taxed annually and rarely match the rate. A common, sensible pattern is a core-and-satellite approach: SSY as the guaranteed core for education, and market investments as a satellite for aspirations beyond the essentials. The calculator supports that conversation by showing exactly what the guaranteed core alone can deliver, so any additional investing is a bonus rather than a rescue mission.

A few practical habits protect the account over its long life. Deposit early in April when possible, or before the fifth of any month for monthly deposits, to earn interest on the full month. Keep the passbook updated and check the credited interest each year. If your family moves, the account can be transferred anywhere in India between post offices and banks, free of charge once a year with proof of residence change. Nominate and update guardian details after any major life event. If a year is missed, revive the account promptly by paying the minimum deposit for each defaulted year plus a small penalty per year, rather than letting it drift. When your daughter turns eighteen, she can operate the account herself, a small financial rite of passage worth celebrating and a natural moment to teach her how compounding built the balance she now sees.

Finally, remember what this calculator can and cannot do. It cannot predict the quarterly rates the government will declare across the next two decades, and it cannot know your family's future income, so its maturity figure is a disciplined estimate, not a guarantee. What it can do is remove vagueness. In one view you can see total deposits, total interest, the year deposits end, the year the account matures, your daughter's age at that moment, an estimate of the education withdrawal available at eighteen, and a complete twenty-one-row grid you can print and pin above a desk. Return to it each year after the interest is credited, adjust the rate, and watch the plan settle. A nest is not built in a day; it is built twig by twig, deposit by deposit, until one day it is simply ready. LittleNest is here, free and unlimited, for every one of those yearly visits.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)?

SSY is a Government of India small-deposit scheme for a girl child. A parent or guardian opens one account for a daughter aged 10 or younger, deposits for 15 years, and the account matures after 21 years, earning interest declared by the government each quarter.

2. How much can I deposit in SSY each year?

You can deposit a minimum of Rs 250 and a maximum of Rs 1,50,000 in a financial year, in multiples of Rs 50. Deposits are required for the first 15 years from the date the account is opened.

3. When does an SSY account mature and what is my daughter's age then?

The account matures 21 years after opening, or at the daughter's marriage after she turns 18, whichever conditions apply. LittleNest shows her exact age at maturity — opening age plus 21 years — right on the results panel.

4. Can I withdraw money early for my daughter's education?

Yes. After your daughter turns 18 or passes Class 10, a partial withdrawal of up to 50 percent of the balance at the end of the preceding financial year is allowed for higher education, once a year for up to five years, against proof of admission and fees.

5. Is SSY interest and maturity tax-free?

SSY enjoys EEE tax status: deposits qualify for deduction under Section 80C up to Rs 1.5 lakh, the interest earned is tax-free, and the maturity amount is also tax-free, subject to prevailing tax rules.

6. How accurate is this SSY calculator?

LittleNest compounds your deposits annually at the rate you enter for the full 21 years. The actual government rate changes every quarter, so your real maturity value will differ slightly. Use the rate slider to test lower and higher scenarios.
